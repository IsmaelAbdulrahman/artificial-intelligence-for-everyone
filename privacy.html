Privacy Policy

Artificial Intelligence for Everyone — Android app

Package iq.edu.epu.aife
Effective date: 1 September 2026  ·  Last updated: 1 September 2026
Published by Dr. Ismael Khorshed Abdulrahman, Erbil Polytechnic University, Erbil, Iraq
Contact: ismael.abdulrahman@epu.edu.iq

In short: this app collects nothing about you.

It asks for no permissions. It has no internet access. Nothing you do in the app is sent anywhere, because there is nowhere for it to be sent. The rest of this page explains that in more detail.

1. Data we collect

None.

The app does not collect, store on a server, transmit, sell, rent, share or otherwise disclose any personal data. Specifically, it does not collect:

  • your name, email address, phone number or postal address
  • any account or user ID — the app has no accounts and no sign-in
  • your location, approximate or precise
  • your contacts, calendar, photos, videos, audio, files or messages
  • your device identifiers, advertising ID, IMEI, MAC address or IP address
  • your browsing history, search history or app usage
  • crash logs, diagnostics or analytics of any kind
  • payment or financial information — the app is free and has no purchases

There is no analytics service, no advertising network, no crash reporting service, no attribution or measurement SDK, and no third-party code of any kind inside the app beyond Google's own AndroidX support libraries, which do not collect data.

The app is not an AI assistant and does not talk to any AI model. It is a book about AI. Nothing you read, search for or type in it is sent to a model, a server or anyone else.

2. Permissions

The app requests no Android permissions at all. Its manifest contains no permission requests of any kind, including no INTERNET permission.

Because the app has no INTERNET permission, the Android operating system prevents it from opening any network connection. It cannot contact a server even by accident, and it could not send your data anywhere if it were asked to.

Exporting or importing your progress uses Android’s own document picker. That needs no permission and grants no browsing rights: the system shows you the chooser, and the app receives only the single file you name or select. It cannot see any other file on your device, before or after.

3. What the app stores on your device

The app saves your reading progress so you can pick up where you left off:

  • which lessons you have read and how far through each chapter you are
  • your bookmarks, both of lessons and of pages in the printed book
  • which practice problems you have tried, and your self-test scores
  • your flashcard review schedule
  • your settings — theme, text size, and where you last were in the book

All of this is written to the app's own private storage on your device, using the standard web localStorage mechanism inside the app's WebView. It stays on your device. No copy is sent to the developer or to anyone else.

You can erase it at any time in two ways: open Settings → Reset progress inside the app, which clears everything; or uninstall the app, which removes all of it along with the app.

4. Backups

If you have Android's own backup or device transfer feature turned on, Android may include this app's saved progress in your personal Google backup, so that your progress follows you to a new phone.

That backup is performed by the Android operating system under your own Google account and your own backup settings. It goes to your own Google account storage. The developer does not perform it, does not receive it and cannot read it. You can turn it off in your device's Android settings, under Backup.

Google's handling of your backup is covered by Google's Privacy Policy, not by this one.

5. Content in the app

The complete text of the book — all 24 chapters, every figure, table and code listing, the practice problems, the self-tests, the flashcards, the glossary and the printed Student Edition PDF — is bundled inside the app when you install it. Nothing is downloaded, streamed or fetched while you use it. The app works exactly the same in aeroplane mode.

Some chapters cite websites and papers as sources. Tapping one of those links opens it in your own browser, outside this app. What happens after that is between you, your browser and the site you opened, and is covered by their privacy policies, not this one.

6. Advertising

The app contains no advertising. It does not use an advertising ID. It does not build a profile of you and does not participate in any advertising network.

7. Children's privacy

The app is written for students, teachers and adults, and its target audience on Google Play is set to 13 and over. It contains no objectionable content, no advertising, no purchases, no chat and no user-generated content.

Because the app collects no data whatsoever, it collects no data from children either. There is nothing for a parent to request access to, correct or delete, and no consent to give, because no personal information is ever gathered from any user regardless of age.

If you are a parent or guardian and you have any question about this, please write to ismael.abdulrahman@epu.edu.iq.

8. Your rights

Data protection laws such as the GDPR give you rights to access, correct, port and erase personal data held about you. The developer holds no personal data about any user of this app, so there is nothing to access, correct, export or erase. The only data connected with the app is the progress stored on your own device, which is entirely under your control and which you can delete yourself at any time as described in section 3.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted at this same address and the effective date at the top will be updated. If a future version of the app ever collected any data, this policy would be updated and the app's Data safety section on Google Play would be updated before that version was released.
